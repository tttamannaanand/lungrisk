      On bad-air days

      Stay indoors when you can, especially in the morning and late evening when pollution often peaks. A well-fitted N95 mask filters most PM2.5; cloth masks don't. If you smoke, your lungs are taking both hits: check your smoking risk, and call the free National Tobacco Quitline on 1800 112 356 for help to stop.

      About the numbers. PM2.5 comes from the Open-Meteo Air Quality API, which uses Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS) forecasts on a grid about 45 km wide. It's a model estimate for the wider area, not a street-level reading, and local monitors (such as CPCB stations) are often higher in busy areas. The cigarette comparison is Berkeley Earth's rule of thumb for health effects, not a medical equivalence. AQI bands follow CPCB's National AQI for PM2.5.
